Sikemux on your phone signs in to the same account as your Mac and reaches every host on it.

## Getting started

Sign in to your Sikemux account in **Settings → Account**, with Google, GitHub or your email. Then open the **Devices** page from **Devices** in the account menu, ⌘⇧H, or **Devices** under **New…**, and choose **Pair device**. Scan the code it shows with your phone's camera, then sign in on the phone with the same account. **Remote access**, beside the page's **Devices** heading, lets your devices reach this host; it turns on when you sign in.

Each phone then has a row under **Devices**, with when it was last connected and what it may do: **Full access** lets it do everything on this host that you can; **Watch** lets it read chats and answer agents' permission requests, nothing more. On the phone, a host where it may only watch says **Watch only** on its card. Its **⋯** switches between the two, **Rename…** renames it on your account, **Remove from this host** takes it off this host only: it waits for you to allow it the next time it asks, even with approval off, and comes back with no more than it had. **Remove from account** signs it out and takes it off every host at once.

Another host's own Sikemux app, once it reaches this one, is not listed with the phones: its host's card on the **Devices** page has an **App** line saying whether it is connected and what it may do, with the same **⋯** less the account actions, since it stays on your account as long as its host does. This host's card says its own app is always allowed.

If something other than Sikemux changes this host's list of devices, the host stops trusting it and pauses remote access. The **Devices** section then names what the list held: **Bring back those on your account** lets in again the devices and hosts your account still has, and **Start over** forgets them.

## Your account is the key

Every device and host signed in to your account reaches every host on it with full access, with nothing to approve: signing in is the proof. Your phone connects to each host on your account by itself; its card on **Devices** reads **Connecting…** until it is in, or says why it could not get in, with **Try again**. A host you forget on the phone stays off it until you tap **Try again**. When a new device or host joins your account, each of your other devices says so, **New device on your account**, with its name; **Remove** takes it off your account and off every host at once.

A host you want to guard more can ask first instead: turn on **Require approval for new devices** for it, in the **⋯** beside it under **Hosts** in the app or on its card's detail on the **Devices** page, or at the top of its **Paired devices** on your phone. A new device or host then waits there, and your phone gets a notification to allow it, for ten minutes, even if the device gives up waiting sooner. A phone waiting on such a host says so on its card: **Waiting for** the host **to allow this phone**.

## Calls and how agents stand

Each agent on a host's **Agents** tab ends its row with how its turn stands, as your Mac's tab rail marks it: the working squares, a pink ring with the minutes left while it waits on CI, an amber dot with how long a call has waited on you, a purple dot once it is done, red once it failed, a hollow ring once it stopped. The **Calls** tab lists what agents wait on you for and what came of what you answered; see [When the agent asks you something](/agents/chats/#when-the-agent-asks-you-something).

## At a glance

While an agent works or needs you, your phone shows it outside the app. On iPhone a Live Activity holds the lock screen and the Dynamic Island: how many agents need you and how many work, then up to three of them, the ones that need you first, each with its title, name and state. Tapping one opens its chat. It follows your hosts while Sikemux is open or was just left, says **May be out of date** once it has heard nothing for fifteen minutes, and goes a few minutes after the last agent stops. Live Activities can be turned off for Sikemux in the iPhone's **Settings → Sikemux**.

Two widgets go on the home screen: **Needs you**, with how many agents wait on you across your hosts and the first of them, and **Limits**, with how much of each agent account's plan is used and when it resets, as your Mac's Limits panel shows it; the small one shows the account nearest its limit. On Android an ongoing notification says which agent needs you, else how many work, until none does, and the **Limits** widget lists each account's windows.

With the lock on, none of these shows what an agent works on or its name, only its host and how many.

## The lock

Sikemux can ask for Face ID, Touch ID or your phone's passcode each time it opens, before it shows any host or chat. It is off until you turn on **Lock Sikemux** in the account sheet, which asks once to check it works; your phone needs a passcode for it.

With the lock on, Sikemux asks again once the app has been in the background for a minute, and while it is away the app switcher shows a blank screen in its place. A link or a notification you tap opens after you unlock. On iPhone, **Allow** or **Reject**, or an answer to a call, on a notification is sent only then; on Android they answer from the notification itself, and **Allow** and a call's answers first ask for the phone's own lock; on Android 11 and older, which can't ask for it there, they open the app instead. Signing out turns the lock off.

## Adding a host with a code

A server or spare computer with no browser signs in with `sikemux login`, which prints a code. To add it, open **Devices → Have a code?** on your phone, or app.sikemux.com/host in a browser, and type the code yourself. A link never fills it in, so nobody can send you one that adds their computer.

Before you add it, Sikemux shows when the host asked and where from: the country and network its address belongs to, then the address, or that the place is unknown. A host once removed from your account says when it was removed. Add only a computer you are setting up right now; **Not mine** stops its sign-in.

Adding a host needs a sign-in from the last ten minutes. If yours is older, Sikemux asks for your password, or a code sent to your email, and then adds the host.
