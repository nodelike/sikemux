title: Mods
description: "Panels you or your agents make as a web page: a pull request queue, CI status, an agent usage meter, anything Sikemux or your connectors know."
---

A **mod** is a panel you make yourself, as a web page, that opens in a tab like any other. It can show what Sikemux knows, such as your agents, the agent board, your processes and Git, and what your signed-in connectors read, such as pull requests, CI runs, Jira issues or logs. Mods live on your host, outside the app, so they cost the download nothing.

## Making one

The easiest way is to ask an agent. Click **+** beside **Mods** in the side rail, or pick **Make a mod with an agent** in **Open mod…**, and say what it should show, such as "my open pull requests with their CI status". An agent starts in the project in front, writes the mod and opens it.

To change a mod, right-click it in the rail and pick **Edit with an agent**.

## Opening one

Each mod has a row under **Mods** in the side rail, with its icon, its name and a few words it may show beside them, such as "3 failing". Click it to open the mod, or to bring its tab forward if it is open. **Open mod…** in the command palette and **Mod** under **New…** list them too.

Right-click a mod's row to:

- **Show only in** the project in front. A mod shows in every project until you do; **Show in every project** brings it back.
- **Edit with an agent**.
- **Remove** it, which moves its folder to the Trash.

**Settings → Mods** lists every mod with what it reads, what it may change and the sites it reaches, and opens, turns off or removes each.

### Examples

Sikemux ships **Pull requests**, which shows the project's open pull requests with their checks and its latest CI runs on GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket or Forgejo, and re-runs a failed one. Add it from **Settings → Mods** or **Open mod…**: it is copied into your mods folder, where you or an agent can change it.

## What a mod may do

A mod reads only what its `mod.json` names, and never sees your sign-ins: Sikemux asks the service and hands the mod the answer.

A mod that changes something, such as messaging an agent, adding a card to the agent board or re-running a CI run, names that in its `mod.json` as well. The first time it tries, Sikemux lists everything it may change and asks you once. **Allow** holds until its `mod.json` asks for more. **Take back approval** in **Settings → Mods** withdraws it.

A mod's page is closed to the internet. It cannot fetch, post a form, open a frame or load a picture or font from anywhere, so what it reads goes nowhere else. A mod that needs a site names it in `connect`, and Sikemux asks you when you open it, listing those sites with everything it may change. Until you allow it, the page stays closed, and the approval lapses whenever its `connect` or its writes change. A link opens in your browser only when you click it.

A mod keeps its own data with no declaration and no asking, since it can reach only its own (see [Keeping data](#keeping-data)). Removing a mod forgets its data too.

A mod whose tab is out of sight stops reading and its timers pause, so a mod left open does not slow your agents.

## Writing one by hand

Each mod is a folder in `~/.config/sikemux/mods/`, named with lowercase letters, digits and dashes, holding two files. Sikemux follows the folder: a new mod shows in the rail at once, and an open mod shows your changes as you save.

`mod.json` says what it is:

```json
{
  "name": "Pull requests",
  "icon": "pull-request",
  "description": "Open pull requests and their CI.",
  "reads": ["repo", "github"],
  "writes": ["github.rerun"],
  "connect": ["https://avatars.githubusercontent.com"]
}
```

`icon` is one of Sikemux's icon names, such as `dashboard`, `pull-request`, `agents`, `board`, `gauge` or `jira`.

`connect` lists the sites the page may fetch from, open a WebSocket to and show pictures from, each as an origin: `https://` or `wss://`, a host, and maybe a port, such as `https://api.example.com:8443`. `https://*.example.com` covers every host under `example.com`, though not `example.com` itself. Anything else, such as a path or `http://`, keeps the mod from showing, and **Settings → Mods** says why. Leave it out and the page reaches nothing.

`index.html` is the page. Keep it one file, with its scripts and styles inline: pictures and fonts beside it are folded in, other files are not, and nothing loads from the internet but what `connect` names. It wears your theme through CSS variables such as `--foreground`, `--muted-foreground`, `--border`, `--accent`, `--success`, `--warning` and `--destructive`, and links open in your browser.

The page reaches Sikemux through `window.sikemux`:

| Call | What it does |
| ---- | ------------ |
| `sikemux.read(name, params)` | Reads `name` once and resolves with the answer. |
| `sikemux.watch(name, params, callback, { every })` | Calls `callback(value, error)` now and whenever the answer changes, only while the mod shows. Sikemux's own data is read again when it changes; a connector is read again every `every` milliseconds, a minute unless set. Returns a function that stops it. |
| `sikemux.write(name, params)` | Makes a change, asking you first as above. |
| `sikemux.badge(text)` | Shows a few words beside the mod's name in the rail, or none with `null`. |
| `sikemux.every(ms, run)` | Runs `run` every `ms` milliseconds while the mod shows. |
| `sikemux.on("shown" \| "hidden", listener)` | Hears the mod's tab come forward or go out of sight. |

What a mod reads, each named in `reads`:

| Name | Answer |
| ---- | ------ |
| `agents` | The project's agents as agents' own `agent_list` gives them; `{ all: true }` for every project. |
| `board` | The project's agent board. |
| `tasks`, `runs` | The project's processes, and their runs. |
| `questions`, `inbox` | What agents ask you, and what waits on you. |
| `usage` | How much of their plans your agent accounts have used. |
| `repo` | The code host the project's origin is on: `connector`, `owner`, `name`, `account`. |
| `git.status`, `git.log`, `git.branches`… | The project's repository, as the Git pane reads it. Declare `git`. |
| `github.pulls`, `gitlab.runs`, `jira_search`… | A connector you are signed in to, by its method or by its agent tool's full name. Declare the connector, such as `github` or `jira`. |

What a mod may change, each named in `writes`: `agents.send` (`{ agent, text }`, as if you wrote it), `board.add`, `board.update`, and a connector's own changes, such as `github.rerun` or `jira_comment`.

## Keeping data

A mod keeps its own documents on your host, apart from its folder, so they stay when the page changes and an edit to the page never loses them. Each document is a JSON object, named by a collection and an id, such as `("posts", "2026-11-13")`.

| Call | What it does |
| ---- | ------------ |
| `sikemux.data.get(collection, id)` | Resolves with the document, or `null`. |
| `sikemux.data.list(collection)` | Resolves with the collection's documents as `[{ id, doc }]`, ordered by id. |
| `sikemux.data.set(collection, id, doc)` | Replaces the document whole, and resolves with it. |
| `sikemux.data.patch(collection, id, patch)` | Changes some of its fields as a JSON Merge Patch: a field set to `null` goes. Makes the document if it has none. Resolves with the document after. |
| `sikemux.data.remove(collection, id)` | Removes the document. |
| `sikemux.watch("data", { collection }, callback)` | Calls `callback(documents)` now and whenever the collection changes, as `list` gives it, from any pane or window. |

Collections and ids are 1 to 64 letters, digits, dashes, underscores or dots, not starting with a dot. Sikemux keeps a record of when each field of a document last changed, and of fields taken out, and that record counts toward its size: a document holds at most 60 KiB with it, and a mod at most 10,000 documents and 16 MiB. Setting a document whole drops the record of fields taken out before. A document with no fields left is removed.
