title: Chats and terminals
description: "An agent's chat, its own terminal UI, and how to talk to it while it works."
---

Start an agent with `⌘N`. Each agent opens in the chat Sikemux draws for it. Its agent starts as you begin writing, so a chat you open and leave empty runs nothing. An agent that went to sleep shows its chat as it was and wakes as you begin writing to it.

## GUI and TUI

The **GUI / TUI** switch at the top of an agent swaps between:

- **GUI**: Sikemux's chat, with the transcript, tool calls, questions and the composer.
- **TUI**: the agent's own terminal interface, running in a Sikemux terminal.

Switch while the agent is idle: while it works, the switch is greyed out until it finishes its reply.

What you typed but did not send comes with you. A draft in the composer waits at the TUI's prompt, unsent, and what is typed in Claude's input box comes back to the composer.

## The composer

- `↑` in an empty box brings back what you sent before, and `↓` goes forward again.
- `/` lists the session's commands and skills; the one you pick goes in as a chip, like a mention. A command the session doesn't have isn't sent: it stays in the draft, and the composer says so.
- `@` names another agent, in this project or any other, or an earlier chat to open again. An agent you renamed keeps its name and buddy after you close it, so `@` finds it by that name and opens its chat again.
- `#` hands over a GitHub, GitLab or Bitbucket issue, and `!` a pull request.
- `+` attaches a file or folder from the project.
- Drag an agent or chat from the tab rail onto a composer to hand it over as context. An open agent goes in as an `@` chip at the cursor, and the agent you are writing to learns how to read what it said; a closed chat opens again for the message, as with `@`. A teammate on another host goes in by its name and host; another host's agents outside your team can't be handed over.
- `&`, or **Stash** beside **+**, opens your [Stash](/getting-started/stash/), what you shared to Sikemux from your phone; the item you pick goes into the message as a chip. `⌘S` stashes the message you are writing for later.
- A picture you paste, drop or add with the `+` button is numbered, and shows up as **Image 1**, with a small copy of the picture, where the caret was. The agent sees each picture at that spot in your message, just as a terminal agent would. Click a picture above the box to see it at full size.
- What you pick stays in your message as a chip. Backspace once selects a chip, and again removes it.
- On your phone, `@`, `#`, `!` and `+` open the same lists above the composer; tap a kind at the top to switch to it. A backspace into a chip there removes it at once.
- The ring beside the model shows how full the agent's context is; hover it for the tokens and what the session has cost. Once Claude Code, Codex or Muse has compacted the conversation to make room, the ring holds how many times, and its card says when it last did. Many compactions in one chat mean the agent has lost detail, so a fresh chat may serve better.
- Selections from terminals, problems and logs can be sent to any agent. They land in its draft, or are typed into its CLI when it runs in its terminal.

## While the agent works

| You press | What happens |
|---|---|
| `Enter` | Sends it now, steering the agent in the middle of its reply |
| `⌘Enter` | Your message waits and is sent when the agent finishes its reply |
| `⌘Enter` in an empty box | Sends the messages that were waiting, now |
| `Esc` in an empty box | Stops the agent's reply |

To swap the two, set **Settings → Chats → Sending while an agent works** to **Queue**. An agent that can't be steered always queues. Sending from your phone follows the same setting.

## Triggers

An agent can set a trigger on its own chat, to be sent a prompt later: once, such as "check the deploy in 20 minutes", or on a schedule, such as every hour or each weekday at 9:00. Ask it to, in your own words.

Its triggers are listed above the composer, each with when it next fires. **×** removes one. When a trigger fires, its prompt arrives in the chat as a message from **Trigger**, and an agent that was asleep wakes for it. A time that passes while the host is off fires once the host is back: a one-time trigger always, a repeating one only within 15 minutes of its time.

Closing the agent pauses its triggers, and a toast says how many with **Undo**, which opens the chat again. **Recent** keeps the chat while they wait, with a clock and their count on its row, and taking it up again, from **Recent** or by an agent resuming it, starts them again: a repeating trigger skips the times that passed while it was closed, and a one-time trigger that came due meanwhile fires once, saying when it was due. A webhook trigger keeps its address, but requests that arrive while the chat is closed are dropped. Paused triggers still count toward the 20 a chat holds. Rest the pointer on that row to see them; **×** beside one removes it for good.

For recurring work that shouldn't depend on one chat, such as triaging new issues every morning, ask the agent to put the trigger on the board. Each time it fires, it adds a card to the project's board as if you added it, so Auto starts an agent on it when Auto is on. While the card it added last is still open, a firing goes to that card instead of adding another, and the agent working it is told, so the same work never starts twice. Board triggers are listed under **Triggers** at the top of the **Agent board**, where **×** removes one; they stay after the agent that set them closes.

## Editing a message you sent

In a Claude Code, Codex, Muse or Pi chat, click the pencil under a message you sent, **Edit message**, while the agent is idle. Change it and press `Enter`; `Esc` leaves it as it was. The chat goes back to that message: the replies after it are dropped, and your new text is sent in its place.

## Subagents

When an agent hands part of its work to subagents, each one shows in the chat as a card with its name, its task and how it stands: working, done, failed, lost or stopped, with its tool calls and tokens. Click a card to see what that subagent did. While any are still working, they are also listed above the composer.

## Messages between agents

When agents on a team message a chat, what they say folds to one line, so your prompt and its answer stay close together. The line shows who wrote, how many messages there are, the latest of them and how long it went on. Click it to open it where it is, on the last five messages; **Show N earlier** brings the rest.

Everything in a turn another agent started folds except that turn's last message, and a message that arrives while the agent answers you folds on its own. Whatever stays in view ends the line, so a long job reads as its answers with one line between each. When someone in a line needs you, because it asked you something, waits on a permission, failed its turn or stopped, the line says so in red until that is settled.

Once your last message has scrolled off the top of the chat, a strip over the transcript says what you asked. Click it to go back to that message.

## Sharing a chat

The share button at the top of a chat makes a read-only link to it. Pick how long the link works, 1 hour, 24 hours or 7 days, and **Create link**. Anyone signed in to Sikemux who has the link can read the chat as it happens, from its start, but can't type in it or answer its questions. They see everything the agent read and ran, including what's in your files, so share with care. While it is shared, the button shows how many people are watching; click it to copy the link again or **Stop sharing**.

## Code in a reply

A code block in a reply shows its language's icon at the top. The wrap button beside it wraps long lines to the chat's width, and the copy button copies the code, showing a tick once it has. A long block scrolls inside itself when a scroll starts over it.

## Pages in a reply

An agent can answer with a page it built, such as a chart, a table or a comparison, drawn right in its reply. Hover the page for **Open in tab**, which opens it on the agent's desk; on Linux it sits under the page. A mockup of a whole screen, app or site opens in a tab on the agent's desk instead, at full width, and the reply shows a card with a picture of it; **Show** brings that tab forward, or opens it again if it was closed. A reply draws at most 4,000 px of a page: a taller one fades out at the cut on a Mac, with how much it shows and **Open in tab** under it. Clicking a page never takes the keyboard from the composer.

## Schemas in a reply

When an agent writes a database schema in a ` ```dbml ` code block, the chat draws it as a diagram of its tables and the references between them. A scroll that starts over it pans the diagram, and one that starts elsewhere keeps scrolling the chat; pinch or `⌘`-scroll zooms, and tables drag. While the agent is still writing it, the diagram only grows, so it holds still between tables. **Fit** shows every table again and **Arrange** lays them out afresh. **Source** shows the DBML, the copy button copies it, and **Open on desk** opens it on the agent's desk, as a diagram with room to spread out and its DBML a click away.

## Diagrams in a reply

When an agent writes a ` ```mermaid ` code block, such as a flowchart, a sequence, class, state or ER diagram, a pie chart or a Gantt chart, the chat draws it as a diagram in your theme's colours. It draws it once the agent has finished writing the block, which shows its text until then. A diagram wider than the chat shrinks to fit. **Source** shows the Mermaid text and **Diagram** the drawing again; the copy button copies the text. A block that isn't a diagram Mermaid can draw stays as text, with **Can't draw** above it, and pointing at that says why. On the phone, a Mermaid block shows as its text.

## Reactions and GIFs

An agent can react to your message with an emoji, which shows on the corner of your bubble, and can send a GIF, which shows where it sent it with **Powered by KLIPY** beneath it. When a turn's work folds away, its GIFs stay in view above the reply. Neither shows up as a step in the chat, and the agent doesn't mention them. A message from another agent can get a reaction too.

## Long chats

A chat holds only the part of its transcript near what you're reading, so a long one stays quick. Scroll up and earlier turns come back as you reach them, and the find bar searches the whole chat. A chat that is asleep, as resumed tabs are until you select them, shows its history, scrolls and finds without starting its agent.

## Agent names

Each agent gets a one-word name when it starts, an anime character's such as Luffy or Frieren, that no other open agent in its project has. To name new agents after nature instead, such as Otter or Maple, set **Settings → Chats → Agent names** to **Nature**. Agents already open keep their names, and so does any agent you renamed.

When you name an agent with `@` in a message, or another agent messages it, a band of light passes once over its row in the tab rail and on the island.

The tab rail lists the agents that need you first, then those at work, each keeping its place while it works, then the finished and idle ones, the one you last wrote to first. An agent working or messaging other agents on its own never moves an idle one up the list.

To change a chat's title, double-click it in its header or in the tab rail, type, and press Return; Escape leaves it as it was. **Edit Title…** in its right-click menu does the same.

## Earlier chats

**Recent** in the tab rail lists the project's earlier chats, newest first. Scroll to its foot and older ones load, a page at a time, and the rail's search finds any of the project's saved chats by title, however old. ⌘⇧N lists them too, beside the agents already open: pinned first, then newest first, and typing narrows the list without reordering it. A chat that ran in a worktree is listed under its project and opens again in that worktree.

## Pinned chats

Point at an open chat in the tab rail and click the pin beside its close, or right-click a chat, open or from the earlier chats, and choose **Pin**. It stays in **Pinned**, right under the running chats, whether it is open or closed, and keeps the name you give it. Click a closed one to open it again. The same pin, or **Unpin** in the menu, takes it off. Drag a pinned chat up or down to put it in another place among them.

Click the heading of **Pinned** or **Recent** to fold it away. A folded group shows how many chats it holds, and stays folded after Sikemux restarts.

## Snoozing and settling

To put a chat out of the way for a while, right-click it and choose **Snooze**: in an hour, in three hours, this evening, tomorrow morning or next Monday, or **Pick a Time…** and type one, such as `fri 2pm`, `tomorrow 8` or `in 90m`. It moves under **Snoozed** in the tab rail, with how soon it wakes, and comes back to the main list at that time, or earlier as soon as its agent asks you something, needs a permission or runs into a problem. **Wake Now** in its menu brings it back at once.

Sikemux settles a chat by itself once you have seen what it last did, it has sat idle for three hours and what it changed is committed; a chat you pinned or are looking at stays. Choose **Settle** in a chat's menu to do the same now. A settled chat moves under **Settled**, folded at the foot of the tab rail, and leaves the island and your phone's list; each settle shows **Undo**. It comes back by itself when you write to it or its agent asks you something. **Settings → Settled chats** lists every settled chat by project, with **Restore** on each, and **Restore** in its menu does the same. Sikemux leaves a lead in the main list while any agent it started is still there.

## Closing an agent

Closing an agent stops it, however you close it: its tab, its row's close in the tab rail, ⌘W, or **Close** and **Close Others** in a menu. When one is at work, Sikemux asks first. When it started other agents, it asks whether they stop too: **Stop them** closes them with it, and **Keep them** leaves them running on their own.

## Links to a chat

Right-click a chat and choose **Copy Link** for a `sikemux://` link that opens it again, in its project, from anywhere on your Mac. If the link names a folder that isn't one of your open projects, Sikemux asks before opening it, since the agent starts there at once and follows that folder's own settings and hooks.

## Moving a chat to another project

Right-click an agent's tab or its row in the tab rail and choose **Move to Project…**, then pick the project. A chat that hasn't started yet can also move from the project name above its composer.

A Claude Code or Codex chat that has already started moves too, with its history: Sikemux stops it, files its saved conversation under the new project, and resumes it there. Wait for it to finish its reply first. Other agents can move only before their first message.

## If something stops it mid-reply

An agent's reply finishes even if Sikemux's background process crashes, your computer restarts, or the agent's own process dies, and nobody has to be watching. Sikemux opens the agent's saved conversation again, says so in the chat, and asks the agent to check what got done before carrying on, since a step cut off mid-way may not have finished. After a few stops in a row the agent is left stopped, with **Retry**.

After a restart this happens when Sikemux's background process starts again: at login when remote access is on, otherwise when you next open Sikemux.

## When the agent asks you something

An agent's questions open above the composer, one tab per question. Pick an answer, or type your own in the composer, and the next question comes up. Nothing reaches the agent until every question has an answer: then **Send** gives it all of them in one message. A single question goes as soon as you answer it. Files and pictures you attach in the composer go with your answer, and reach the agent as attachments, the same as with a message. On the island, drop a file on **Answer** to answer the question waiting with it.

| You press | What happens |
|---|---|
| `1`–`9` | Picks that answer, or ticks it when the question takes several |
| `↑` `↓` | Moves between the answers |
| `Space` | Ticks the answer you moved to, when the question takes several |
| `←` `→` | Moves between the questions |
| `Enter` | Picks the answer you moved to, or moves on once you've ticked some |
| `⌘Enter` | Sends every answer, once each question has one |

When an agent you started yourself asks you something in the reply its turn ends with, anywhere in it and not only at the end, such as "Which of the two names do you want?", Sikemux makes that a question too: it shows with the questions, and your answer goes back to the agent as a message. Sikemux takes a sentence ending in a question mark as a question when it ends its paragraph or list item, or when all that follows it there is what the agent would pick or that it waits for your pick, as in "Which one? I'm leaning A. I won't start until you pick.", and leaves out code, quotes, headings and tables; a sentence never ends inside bold, italics or code, and the question shows without their marks. A last sentence that means nothing alone, such as "Thoughts?" or "Is that okay?", shows the latest real question before it in the reply, or with none, the paragraph it closes. That paragraph is a question to you when it also asks something a yes or a no can't answer, such as "Who owns this?". A reply that asks several things, one per paragraph or list item, makes a question of each, one tab apiece, the way an agent's question tool asks several at once. Your answers go back together once each question has one, in one message that puts every answer beside the question it answers. If you write to the agent before answering them all, the answers you gave go with your message, each shown under it with its question, and the questions you left no longer wait. Each agent's questions wait on you as one batch, the latest, whether it asked with its question tool or in its reply; the inbox, Goals and your phone show them one at a time, in the order the agent asked them. It counts in the inbox and on the Dock badge until you answer it, and goes away once the agent starts another turn, because you folded the questions away and wrote to it or for any other reason. Agents another agent leads, and those Auto runs, answer to them instead. Three kinds go back to the agent once instead of reaching you: a question in a turn another agent's message started, which the two agents settle between them, one that says nothing without the chat, such as "Keep them or delete them?" naming nothing, and a choice left to you in the reply, such as "Want me to cache it, or drop it?" or "Say A or B", which agents must ask with their question tool. The agent decides it, or asks you again so that it stands on its own; asking again right after reaches you. A choice that still comes only in the reply shows under **Needs you** with the options it named to tap, such as **A** and **B** for mockups it laid out as A and B, and the one the agent said it would pick.

An offer a yes or a no answers, such as "Want me to commit it?" or "Ship it to dev?", is not a question to you. If one of the project's [rules](/agents/memory/) names what it offers, Sikemux sends the agent back once and the rule decides: the agent does the work if the rule says to, and leaves it if the rule says to wait for you. Otherwise, and in that case too, the agent shows how its turn left things, such as **Done, uncommitted** on its row in Goals with the offer under it, and you take it up in its chat if you want it.

When an agent gives its choices pictures, such as mockups, they show as tiles in the chat under its reply, two to a line, and the box above the composer shrinks to one line saying what you picked. Click a tile or press its number to pick it, and click the picked tile to see its picture full size. A pick waits for **Send** or `Enter`, so you can change it first. If you scroll away from the tiles, **Show choices** brings you back to them. Once you answer, the chat keeps the picture you picked beside your answer.

These keys work while the composer is empty. The arrow at the right of the tabs folds the questions away, so the composer writes to the agent again.

On your phone the questions sit at the end of the chat, with the same tabs. Tap an answer to pick it, or to tick it when the question takes several, and **Send** under the answers sends them all once each question has one. **Something else…** writes your own answer in the composer. The arrow beside who's asking folds them into one line; tap it to open them again.

## Its desk

Each agent has its own browser tabs on its desk. `⌘J` shows or hides the desk of the agent in front. See [Browser](/browser/desk-tabs/).
