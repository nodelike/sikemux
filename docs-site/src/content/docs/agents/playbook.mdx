---
title: The Playbook
description: "What you decided once for a project, which every later agent goes by, and what runs there with nobody asking."
---

Each project has a Playbook: open it from **Playbook** at the end of the project's tree in the side rail, or with `⌘⌥6`. It holds what you decided once that every later agent goes by. Nothing on it has to be set before work starts: agents fill it in as you talk to them, and Sikemux reads the rest from the repository.

Every line says who said it and where. **Open** jumps to that moment in the chat, opening the chat again if it has closed, and **Undo** takes it back.

Its pages sit above the project's settings, which a hairline parts from them.

## Rules

**Your rules** are kept from your own words. When you tell an agent how agents should always or never work here, it keeps that as your rule without asking, and the chat shows one quiet line, **Kept as a rule · Undo**. Only you change these. An agent may remove or reword one only in a turn your own words started, and never the rule an Overrule kept. A line then says **Rule removed** or **Rule changed** with **Undo**, in the project's @Chief chat, or in that agent's chat when the project has no chief. A reword stays your rule only when the new words are yours; otherwise your rule is set aside and the new words are the agent's note. When you repeat a correction one of your rules already makes, the @Chief may have a worker land a check that catches it, such as a lint or a CI step, and take the rule off the list; its chat shows **Now a check · Undo**. Undo brings the rule back as it was, and so does asking an agent to. A rule an Overrule kept never becomes a check, and none of this moves a level or a gate.

**Noted by agents** lists what agents noticed while working, and the knowledge Sikemux keeps of how things are. It guides agents, and never widens what they may do. **Remove** drops a note. [What Sikemux remembers](/agents/memory/) says how agents are handed both.

## Places

Where agents' actions can change something outside your Mac, and which of those wait for you. [Permissions](/agents/permissions/#what-leaves-this-machine) says how Sikemux reads what an action touches.

**Production places** lists the ones Sikemux found in the project's deploy config, its workflows and what agents ran, each with its provider's mark, where it was found and how its calls went, such as "Found in vercel.json · 5 calls, all 5 answered as picked". Where the release workflow tells nightly tags from the rest, nightly and stable releases are two places. Until you look, the list reads **found by Sikemux, not confirmed yet** and a change to any of them waits for your yes. Check it, add what is missing with **Add a place** (a website, a command, a Kubernetes context, an AWS profile, a Terraform workspace, a Rundeck job or a package registry), and press **Looks right**.

Each production place has a level, a word with a menu:

| Level | What agents do there |
| --- | --- |
| **Yours** | They ship there and report after |
| **Tell me** | They ship, and you're told the moment they do |
| **Mine** | They prepare it, and you say yes on a call |

Only your own tap changes a level, and only once the list is confirmed. A level you set says so under the place, with when and where, and **Undo** puts back the one before.

**Other places** are the ones that aren't production, such as staging, where agents deploy without asking.

**Seen, not gated** lists what agents sent out that nothing could place, once each however often it ran, with how often and which agent sent it last. **Count as production** makes it a production place at **Mine**. Past 64 the list says how many more there were rather than dropping what you haven't looked at.

## Landing

**Who lands** says who puts work on the project's branch: agents, once their checks pass, or you. The line under each chat ends a turn with these words, such as **Done · landed · checks pass**.

## Automation

What runs in the project with nobody asking, one line each: the triggers agents set up when you asked, such as "when main's checks fail, find the commit that broke them", and agents starting by themselves on cards nobody holds. Each line says when it next runs and who set it up. **Pause** stops it until you **Resume** it; a trigger that came due while paused runs once when you resume it, and a repeating one skips the times it missed. The bin removes a trigger.
