A goal is a card at the top of the [agent board](/agents/board/) with slices under it. A slice at the top, with no goal above it, such as a card Auto runs, is a goal of its own with one slice. **Goals** is a view of its own in the middle of the window: it puts what needs you first, then each goal and its slices, for the project you pick. Switch to it with the goals button at the top right of the window, after the clock and beside focus mode, or press `⌘⌥7`; press either again to go back to the workbench. The window keeps whichever you left it on.

Both rails stay. The side rail keeps your projects, hosts and the rest, with **Home** and **Overnight** at its top, each with how many things need you or happened while you were away; picking a project there shows that project's goals. The rail on the right lists only that project's goals, each with the mark of where it stands and how many things in it need you; click one to open it.

## Starting work

The composer at the bottom of Home, the same one a chat has, asks what should get done in the project in front of the workbench. Write as you would to an agent: add files with **+**, paste screenshots, dictate with the microphone, and use the up arrow to bring back what you started before. Pick how it starts with the switch at the end of its bar; hover a choice to see what it does. **Hand off** gives it to an agent that works on it on its own, out of sight, **Plan first** adds a goal and starts a lead that talks it through with you and proposes slices for you to approve, and **Chat** starts a chat with you, kept as a goal. The composer opens on **Chat**. While you type, Sikemux switches to **Plan first** when the text calls for it and marks it with a dot; hover it to see why, such as when the text touches server code, a migration, the protocol, what people see, or a path your project lists under [`planFirst`](/getting-started/projects/). Otherwise it stays on **Chat**; it never picks **Hand off** for you. You can always pick another. The agent menu picks which agent starts, its account and its model. Enter or the send arrow starts it, and the agent gets what you wrote with your files. That agent holds its card from the start.

**Hand off** starts its agent in the background, in a checkout of its own cut from where the project is now, and keeps its chat out of your way: the work shows under **Moving** on Home, and under **Needs you** when it asks you something, its turn fails, or it waits at a gate only you pass. Its card is a slice whose promise is what you typed, cut to one line of 200 characters if it is longer, with the whole text in its details. The agent commits and lands its work on the branch it was cut from, and the slice is **Built** once the project's [check](/getting-started/projects/#the-projects-check) passes on it there, which Sikemux runs itself. A project that names no check has nothing to show the work **Built**, so the agent asks you how it is to be checked and the slice waits at **Built** until it has one. Open the agent's chat from its line under **Moving** whenever you want to look in.

## What needs you

Goals opens on **Home**, laid out like a new chat: the agent asks what you're making in the project you picked, with what needs you in any project and what is moving in this one under it, and the composer at the bottom. **Waiting on you** comes first: one line for each agent, in any project, that ended its turn asking you something, with the question, the agent, its project and how long it has waited. When the question takes a short answer, such as an agent offering to do more, its answers sit on the line and one click sends yours; click anywhere else on the line to open the agent's chat and reply in your own words. **Needs you** lists the rest of what waits on you in any project, grouped by what you're needed for: **Decide** (a gate to pass, a permission, a pull request, or a ticket closed with nothing landed), **Unblock** (a sign-in, a secret, a terminal waiting for you to type) and **Fix** (a failed check or something held). Each line names its goal or agent, its project and how long it has waited, and opens the slice, bringing its project to the front, or the agent's chat. The count beside **Home** counts the same things. **Moving** lists the goals with work under way, each with a strip of its slices, and **Done** the goals whose slices have all passed. Click a goal to open it.

Whatever comes to need you also tells you, once: a notification on your Mac while Sikemux isn't in front, and on your phone as its notification settings say. That covers a gate to pass, a card handed back, agents stuck on each other, knowledge that no longer holds and a turn that ends asking you something, besides the permissions, questions and problems that always did. It doesn't tell you again about the same thing, or while you have that agent open, and the notification goes away once it no longer needs you.

When things finished or failed while you were away, a line above what needs you says how many, and opens **Overnight**: how many finished, failed and wait on you, then each event in time order, grouped by goal. Each event opens its slice or its agent's chat.

## A goal

The goal shows who leads it and which agents own its slices, a strip with one segment per slice, and **Next**: the gates its slices wait at, in the order they will be reached. **Needs you** lists the slices waiting at a gate only you pass. **Slices** lists every slice with what it waits on, its ticket and its owner.

**Assertions** lists what must hold once the goal is done, each with the kind of evidence that shows it (the check passing, a recording, a screenshot, or a CI workflow passing, named on the line as "CI Build passes") and how many of the slices that fulfil it have that evidence. Add one on the line under them, picking its kind; **CI workflow** asks for the workflow's name. Retire one with **×**.

**Slices land** sets who passes Landed in the project: **Once on the branch**, which Sikemux passes, or **When you pass it**, where nothing counts as landed until you pass Landed yourself ([when slices land](/agents/board/#when-slices-land)).

**Rules** lists the rules the goal's agents follow: the goal's own, then those Sikemux keeps for the project and the folders above it ([what Sikemux remembers](/agents/memory/)). **Move to AGENTS.md** moves a rule kept for the project into its repository's `AGENTS.md`, where every agent reads it with the code, and Sikemux stops keeping it. The file is left uncommitted for you to review. The button shows only on a rule that can move: never a sensitive one, which never goes into the repository, nor one kept for a folder that isn't the top of a git checkout, or whose `AGENTS.md` links outside it.

Every slice wears the same mark: solid green when done, a green ring while it moves, a dashed grey ring while it waits, amber when it needs you and red when its latest check failed. A goal wears the mark of its slices together: red when one failed, amber when one needs you, a green ring when one moves, solid green when every one is done, and a dashed grey ring otherwise.

## What a slice waits on

You never have to ask an agent for its status. Next and each slice's row say what the slice waits on now, and roughly how long that takes:

- **the project's check** on its owner's latest commit, here or in CI: queued, then running, such as "check · ~3 min" or "CI Release · ~6 min"
- **another card** it waits on, such as "waits for #4"
- **you**, at a gate only you pass
- **its owner**, in the middle of a turn

A check's estimate comes from how long the project's last few checks took since Sikemux last started; there is none until one has run. A wait that runs past its estimate says "over" instead, such as "check · over 3 min". The pane changes the moment a wait starts, ends or runs over.

## A slice

Click a slice to open it. Under its promise and owner, the ladder shows each gate with who passed it and when, or who may pass it and what it still needs. Under the ladder, each of the goal's assertions the slice fulfils is ticked once its evidence held up; an assertion it named that no longer stands on its goal, such as one retired, shows struck through as freed. Each thumbnail of evidence says which assertions it was collected for, by number.

The evidence for the gate waiting leads, large, a picture first when there is one; the rest sit as thumbnails under it, and clicking one brings it to the front. Clicking a picture or recording opens it in the viewer. The checks Sikemux ran fold into one line.

When the slice waits on you, **Looks right** passes the gate. **Needs work** opens a line to say what is wrong; the note is pinned to the evidence in front and goes to the agent whose work it shows. Notes are listed under the buttons, newest first.
