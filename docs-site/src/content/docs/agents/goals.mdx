A goal is a card at the top of the [agent board](/agents/board/) with an agent working it or slices under it. A chat is not a goal, but it can [pick one up](#a-chat-picks-up-a-goal) as it goes. A slice at the top, with no goal above it, such as a card Auto runs, is a goal of its own with one slice. **Goals** is a view of its own in the middle of the window: it puts the calls only you can make first, then every goal and agent on this host. Switch to it with the goals button at the top right of the window, after the clock and beside focus mode, or press `⌘⌥7`; press either again to go back to the workbench. The window keeps whichever you left it on.

Both rails stay. The side rail keeps your projects, hosts and the rest, with **Home** and **Overnight** at its top, with how many things need you or happened while you were away. On Home, picking a project there narrows the list to it; the **×** on its name beside the find box shows every project again. On Home the rail on the right shows [the row you chose](#the-row-you-chose). Elsewhere in Goals it lists the goals of the project in front, each with the mark of where it stands and how many things in it need you; click one to open it.

## Starting work

The composer at the bottom of Home, the same one a chat has, asks what should get done in the project in front of the workbench. Write as you would to an agent: add files with **+**, drop files, folders or pictures anywhere on Home, paste screenshots, mention agents with `@`, issues with `#`, pull requests with `!` and files with `+`, dictate with the microphone, and use the up arrow to bring back what you started before. Open the **Stash** from its bar to bring an item into what you are writing. What you write is kept as you type, so it is still there after a restart. Slash commands belong to a chat's session, so they show in the chat once it starts. Pick how it starts with the switch at the end of its bar; hover a choice to see what it does. **Hand off** gives it to an agent that works on it on its own, out of sight, **Plan first** adds a goal and starts a lead that talks it through with you and proposes slices for you to approve, and **Chat** starts a chat with you and adds nothing to the board. The composer opens on **Chat**. While you type, Sikemux switches to **Plan first** when the text calls for it and marks it with a dot; hover it to see why, such as when the text touches server code, a migration, the protocol, what people see, or a path your project lists under [`planFirst`](/getting-started/projects/). Otherwise it stays on **Chat**; it never picks **Hand off** for you. You can always pick another. The rest of the bar and the strip above it work as they do on a chat that has not started, and what you pick applies when the agent starts: **YOLO** or **SAFE**, the model and its effort, the project, the account, and whether it starts in a worktree of its own. **Hand off** always takes its own, so the worktree switch shows on and fixed while it is picked. Enter or the send arrow starts it, and the agent gets what you wrote with your files and mentions. The chat shows only what you wrote: what Sikemux tells a lead about its card goes with the message unseen, and again if you edit it. An agent given a card holds it from the start. **Plan first** and **Chat** open the new chat right here in Goals.

**Hand off** starts its agent in the background, in a checkout of its own cut from where the project is now, and keeps its chat out of your way: the work shows among the goals on Home, and as one of your calls when it asks you something, its turn fails, or it waits at a gate only you pass. Its card is a slice whose promise is what you typed, cut to one line of 200 characters if it is longer, with the whole text in its details. The agent commits and lands its work on the branch it was cut from, and the slice is **Built** once the project's [check](/getting-started/projects/#the-projects-check) passes on it there, which Sikemux runs itself. It asks you to look only when the work [changes what people see](#looking-once-at-a-goal). A project that names no check has nothing to show the work **Built**, so the agent asks you how it is to be checked and the slice waits at **Built** until it has one. Open the agent's chat from the row you chose whenever you want to look in. Right after you hand it off, the line under the composer names its card for a few seconds; **Open** shows it.

## What reaches you

You don't decide for every agent. A lead decides for the agents it started, and only what is yours reaches you:

- anything irreversible or leaving your Mac: a push, merge, deploy, publish or production write, and permission prompts
- spending money
- a call of taste or product direction
- access only you have, such as a sign-in or a secret

A lead that raises one of these has to say what is being decided, the options, the one it would pick and what breaks if that is wrong, or Sikemux refuses it, so its card tells you what you are deciding without opening a chat. Anything else stays with the team. A lead's answer is kept, with who decided and why. A question a worker went on without keeps its assumption if its lead doesn't answer. A worker that waits on the answer goes back to its lead once, and reaches you only if the lead still doesn't answer, marked as something the lead didn't answer. An agent that no lead started asks you directly. One that goes on with an assumption just keeps it, unless what it asks is yours: it gives a reason, offers choices, or names a push, merge or deploy. That waits with your calls, saying what the agent goes on assuming until you answer.

### Overruling a decision

What the team decided is under **Decided** on its goal's page, and **Overnight** lists it under **Decided without you**, each with who decided and why, leaving out what you chose yourself in answer to an agent, which Sikemux keeps as yours when the agent records it in the turn your answer started: a goal's since you were last away, and those of agents on no goal for as long as they stand, as no other page lists them. **Overrule** retires it, tells the agent that goes by it to stop and undo what rests on it, and opens that agent's chat so you can say what you want instead. On a goal's page, click the decision first to see **Overrule**. A decision never passes a hold: a lead's answer doesn't let anything through that stops for you.

## A chat picks up a goal

A goal is a piece of work a chat takes on, not the chat itself: one chat can hold several over its life, and finishing a goal leaves the chat going. Chats start without one. You never have to decide when a chat becomes a goal: its agent does. When a chat you started has plainly grown into real work, Sikemux tells its agent so with your next message, out of your sight, with what it counted, such as 4 commits, 1 teammate and 42 minutes of work. The agent tracks it as a goal if it is one piece of work with an end, adding a slice it holds with a title and a promise, so it has gates like any other slice and shows among the goals on Home, and otherwise carries on. Sikemux looks once a turn has finished, counting commits, teammates started, subagents and long turns, over at least ten minutes of work unless a teammate was started. A subagent counts for least, since agents start them for small questions. Quick chats never get this, nor do agents another agent started, nor a chat that already has a goal. Once told, an agent isn't told again until about twice as much work has happened.

When a chat's agent tracks its work, the line under the composer says **Tracking as** the goal's name, with **Undo**, which takes the card away again until the next turn begins or its [lease](#when-an-owner-stalls) lapses. The agent hears that you took it back with your next message, so it starts no turn of its own. What the agent committed before it added the goal counts as the slice's work.

While a chat has a goal, the composer's strip shows it at the right end: the goal's mark, its name, a strip of its progress and how far along it is, such as "2 of 3 gates". Click it to open the goal. The chat's transcript marks where each goal began and ended, with a quiet line such as "Goal started: Keep a terminal at two columns". When the goal finishes, the strip clears, the line stays, and the chat's agent can pick up a new one once more work has happened.

## Home

Goals opens on **Home**: only what needs you, then one line for each goal, over the composer. Its top line says how many calls wait for you, such as **3 calls for you**, and under it, quietly, how many agents are working on how many goals and when all of them should be through, such as "everything through by 16:40, then you" when a gate only you pass comes after. The find box beside it narrows everything by a goal's title, what a call asks or an `@agent`. Press `/` to reach the box, `Esc` to empty it, `j` and `k` or the arrows to move through the list and `Enter` to open what you are on. Any other key starts typing in the composer.

Under it comes a card for each thing that needs you, in the order Sikemux ranks them. A card's top line says what kind of call it is, such as **Look**, **Product**, **Money**, **Access**, **Can't be undone** or **Leaves this Mac**, which goal it is on, or its project, and who asks, with their agent's mark. Under that is the question in full; a long one shows its first lines, with **Show all** for the rest. Then you answer it right there, at the card's bottom right, with one button standing out as the likeliest and a quiet hint beside them:

- An agent's question with short answers, such as **Yes** or **No**, lists its answers as rows; click one to answer. The option the asker would pick comes first, lifted and marked **lead's pick** or **agent's pick**, and when a lead raised it the hint says what breaks if that is wrong, as "If wrong: …". When the agent asked several things, the card then shows the next, and the answers go back together once each has one. Any other question has **Answer**, which opens the chat at the question.
- A goal waiting for you to [look](#looking-once-at-a-goal) shows one large pair of pictures side by side, how the page was before its work and how it is now, each filling its frame from the top like a screenshot, then a chip for each slice it covers and, when they were taken, a note that it was also taken at a phone's and a tablet's width. **All N pictures** shows the rest. Each picture shows full size on a click. The card ends with **Looks right** and **Needs work…**, which opens a line on the card to say what needs work. The note goes to the goal; click a picture first to pin it to that picture, and it goes to the agent whose work the picture shows. `Esc` or **Cancel** closes the line. Any other gate only you pass has **Approve**, or **Pass** and its environment, and **See evidence**.
- An agent's push, deploy or other action that [leaves this machine](/agents/permissions/#what-leaves-this-machine) shows its exact command, with the environment it lands in first, and the hint says why it waits. It has **Refuse**, **Allow once**, and **Always allow** where Sikemux [offers it](#environments), which then leads. The buttons wrap rather than run off a narrow window.
- Something failed has **See why**, which opens the failed slice or the agent's chat. A sign-in, a secret or a terminal waiting for you to type has **Unblock**, and anything else has **Review**.
- An owner that stalled has **Resume** and **Hand on**.

A question from an agent on no goal shows once, as its card, and not again as the agent's line.

**Goals** has one line for each open goal: its mark, its name with a plain phrase under it saying what moves or what it waits on, such as "@Faris at work" or "waits on your call", a slim dot for each of its slices in the colour of its mark, and at its end when it should be through, such as "16:20", or its project. A goal whose work rests with one agent, such as a chat you tracked, says whether that agent is at work or idle. An agent working with no goal, such as a chat you started from Home, has a line among them, with its mark: a green ring while it works, and what its turn left, such as **Done, uncommitted**. Idle chats and finished goals fold into one quiet line at the end, such as "28 idle chats · 4 done"; click it to list them. Finding something lists them too.

Click a card or line to choose it, and click it again quickly to open it: a goal's page, or for a goal one agent works alone, that agent's chat, unless the agent closed. Right-click one for the same menu its agent's tab has: rename, sleep, close, share and the rest. The count beside **Home** counts what waits on you, as the cards do.

## The row you chose

On Home, the rail on the right shows what you chose, without repeating its card. For a goal: its title and what comes next, its slices as a line when it has more than itself, and each agent with its task, its `@name` and what is out on its desk. A task that is the goal's own title shows as the agent's `@name` alone. For a call: where it is from, the agent asking with what is out on its desk, and **Open chat**; for rules offered from past sessions, the rules to keep.

An action that leaves this machine and a gate only you pass show in that agent's chat too, as a card above its composer: the exact command with **Refuse**, **Allow once** and, where offered, **Always allow**, or the evidence the gate leads with beside the gate's own button, such as **Pass Production** or a goal's **Looks right**. Answer in either place and it leaves both.

**Focus** goes to the workbench with the agent rail narrowed to this goal's agents; the row at the top of the rail takes that away. **Open** opens the goal's page. Click a slice to open it, an agent to open its chat, or one of the chips under an agent to bring that terminal, page, simulator or file to the front on its desk.

An agent's chat opens right here in Goals, the same live chat as the agent's tab in the workbench, which stays open; the link at the top of the chat, `Esc` once no turn is left to stop, or `⌘W` goes back to where you were, and `⌘⌥7` shows that tab in the workbench. Anywhere else in Goals `⌘W` does nothing, since no tab is showing to close. An agent that runs in a terminal has no chat, so it opens its tab in the workbench, which takes Goals' place, as the board does; `⌘⌥7` brings Goals back.

Whatever comes to need you also tells you, once: a notification on your Mac while Sikemux isn't in front, and on your phone as its notification settings say. That covers a gate to pass, a card handed back, an owner that stalled, agents stuck on each other, knowledge that no longer holds and a turn that ends asking you something, besides the permissions, questions and problems that always did. It doesn't tell you again about the same thing, or while you have that agent open, and the notification goes away once it no longer needs you.

When things finished, failed or were decided while you were away, a link beside the find box says how many, counting only what happened between leaving and coming back, and opens **Overnight**: how many finished, failed, were decided without you and wait on you, then the project's [codebase health](#codebase-health), then each event in time order, grouped by goal, then **Decided without you**. Each event opens its slice or its agent's chat.

### Codebase health

A drift audit is an agent with fresh eyes, one that never saw the work, that reads what landed since the audit before and reports how the codebase holds up: dead code, the same logic written twice, docs and design notes that no longer match the code, rules the code breaks, checks weakened. It changes nothing. Audits are off until you turn them on for a project, since each one spends on a model. Under **Codebase health** on **Overnight**, pick when they run: **Every day at 09:00**, **Every 12 hours** or **Every 6 hours**; **Audit now** runs one at once. Beside the setting it says which agent and model they run on, and who pays: the ones set for Auto's agent on the board. The audit starts at the project's root, on a card of its own titled **Drift audit**, and files each problem as a card under it, where you or Auto can take it up. The digest shows how many commits it read, its findings, each opening its card, how many findings of earlier audits were fixed since, and its few sentences on the codebase as a whole.

## A goal

The goal's page starts with its project, its name and when it should be through, such as "through by 16:20, then you", its ticket with a link to it in its tracker, and a button that opens it beside the [agent board](/agents/board/), where everything about its card can be changed. Under that, who leads it, whose name opens their chat, how many slices it has and when it started. Then, side by side, or one under the other in a narrow window:

- **What happened** says in a short paragraph where the work stands, written from what the goal's agents really did. What failed, was skipped or isn't proven is said as plainly as what passed. A line under it says what Sikemux checked it against, such as gates and evidence.
- **Decided** lists the decisions recorded on the goal, each with who decided, such as "you, overruling @Faris" when you corrected an agent's, an agent's with **Overrule** once you open it, then any a model found in what was said, marked **Extracted**. An extracted decision is only a suggestion until you open it and pick **Keep as a decision**.
- **Slices** lists every slice with its mark, its ticket and where it stands in a word or two: done, you, failed, the slice it waits for, such as "after #4", or who holds it and when it should be through, such as "@Faris · 16:10". Open a slice for the gate it is at and what that needs.

Under them, **Your calls** has a card for each thing that waits on you about this goal, as on Home, including its one [look](#looking-once-at-a-goal).

Each sentence of **What happened** underlines under the pointer; click one, or a decision, to see the moments behind it: what you or an agent said, a commit, a check Sikemux ran, a gate, a piece of evidence or a pull request. Each can be opened: the chat, the commit in Git, the evidence or the pull request. When the goal's agents or cards name a pull request, Sikemux asks the code host where it stands as the page opens, so a merged or closed one is said to be, whatever an agent last wrote; if it can't ask, the summary says it doesn't know. Every sentence names its moments, and Sikemux drops any that names none, or names a moment or a commit that isn't there. If nothing is left, the page says the summary failed and offers **Try again**, rather than guessing. A line under the sentences says when Sikemux itself checked nothing, so they rest on what the agents said.

A summary is written when you open a goal's page and the goal has changed since the last one, never on a timer. The page shows the last one at once, with how old it is, and replaces it when the new one is ready. It is written by the goal's own kind of agent, Claude or Codex, on the lead's account, with no tools and no saved session. Words from an agent of another kind stay out of it, and the page names who wasn't read. A goal led by any other agent gets no summary and says why. What a model reads is cut down first, without pasted text or anything that may be a secret. It is a model's reading, so a sentence can still get its sources wrong; that is what opening them is for.

When a goal's title is long or only the start of what you typed, it gets a short name of a few words as soon as it's made, asked of the agent working on it, on that agent's account. The page, the rail, Home and the questions it puts to you go by that name; until it arrives they show the first clause of what you typed, and its **Looks right** card asks "Does this look right?" with that clause in the line above. If the agent can't be asked, the title its chat gets is used instead. A goal still without a short name when Sikemux starts, such as one made before names were given, is named then. The card keeps its title.

### Looking once at a goal

You look at a goal's work once, not slice by slice, and only when it changes what people see: a slice whose change touches the files a browser draws a page from, such as `.tsx`, `.vue`, `.html` or `.css`, or one that holds a screenshot or recording. A goal with no such slice never asks you to look, and neither does a **Hand off** whose work shows nothing.

The look is due once every such slice is **Built**. Nothing waits for it: the goal's other slices, and these ones, go on landing meanwhile, and the goal counts as done only once you have looked. It comes as one card under **Your calls** for the goal, with every picture and recording its slices hold: those taken before a slice's first commit and those on its latest, so you see before and after where both exist, each at its own width and at a phone's and a tablet's.

**Looks right** passes it. **Needs work…** opens a line to say what is wrong. A goal with a lead sends the note to the lead as new work, which it adds or reopens slices for, and the goal waits on the lead. A goal with no lead of its own, such as a **Hand off**, sends its slices back to their owners, and they climb again from Built once the owner marks them done again. Either way you are asked to look again once something you looked at is built again, or a new slice that shows something is Built.

Last comes a quiet **Details** line; click it for **Assertions**, **Slices land** and **Rules**.

**Assertions** lists what must hold once the goal is done, each with the kind of evidence that shows it (the check passing, a recording, a screenshot, a CI workflow passing, named on the line as "CI Build passes", or a command passing) and how many of the slices that fulfil it have that evidence. Add one on the line under them, picking its kind; **CI workflow** asks for the workflow's name and **Command** for the command. Retire one with **×**.

A command is how you gate on a tool no plugin covers. Sikemux runs it in a clean checkout of each slice's latest commit, apart from the project's check, with the same limits of 30 minutes and 4 MiB of output, and keeps what it printed. Its exit code is the verdict, and no model judges it. Each new commit runs it again, and so does the slice's owner marking it done again. A run that doesn't finish gives no verdict and doesn't count.

A command you write counts at once. One an agent writes doesn't run or count until you accept that exact command: it shows as a card under **Your calls** and under its assertion, in full, with **Accept** and **Turn down**, and any slice that fulfils it says it waits for you. Changing the command asks again. Turning down a change keeps the command you last accepted; turning down a new one retires its assertion. Only the app can answer, so an agent can't accept its own.

Accepting a command also accepts the repository files it names, as they stood when it was written, and a command you write yourself holds its files the same way. If a slice's latest commit holds one of those files differently, adds a file by a name the command uses, or removes one, the command doesn't run on that commit and you're asked again, naming the file and the commit. Accept lets it run; **Turn down** keeps it from running on that content until the slice puts back what you accepted, and you aren't asked about that content again; the slice says you turned it down. This compares git's ids for the content, so no model judges it, but it only sees plain paths in the command. A command that names no repository file, such as `pnpm notes:check`, or a folder, a script run from another folder, or anything a named file calls, can still change without asking. Accept those for what they could become.

**Slices land** sets who passes Landed in the project: **Once on the branch**, which Sikemux passes, or **When you pass it**, where nothing counts as landed until you pass Landed yourself ([when slices land](/agents/board/#when-slices-land)).

**Rules** lists the rules the goal's agents follow: the goal's own, then those Sikemux keeps for the project and the folders above it ([what Sikemux remembers](/agents/memory/)). **Move to AGENTS.md** moves a rule kept for the project into its repository's `AGENTS.md`, where every agent reads it with the code, and Sikemux stops keeping it. The file is left uncommitted for you to review. The button shows only on a rule that can move: never a sensitive one, which never goes into the repository, nor one kept for a folder that isn't the top of a git checkout, or whose `AGENTS.md` links outside it.

Every slice wears the same mark: solid green when done, a green ring while it moves, a dashed grey ring while it waits, amber when it needs you and red when its latest check failed. A goal wears the mark of its slices together: red when one failed, amber when one needs you, a green ring when one moves, solid green when every one is done, and a dashed grey ring otherwise. A slice moves only while something moves it: its owner works, or a check it asked for runs. Once its owner sits idle it waits, and its goal's line on Home says so. A goal with no slices wears its agent's, as [below](#when-an-owner-stalls).

## When an owner stalls

Holding a slice is a lease, so the team never waits in silence on an agent that has gone. A slice loses its owner when the agent's chat closes, or when the agent has run no turn and left the slice untouched for 30 minutes, whether it sits idle, asleep or stopped. It keeps its gates and its work, and anyone can take it up: another agent on the team, or Auto where it takes the card. With **Recover** on, Auto takes it on without asking and carries on in the worktree its owner left, and what it did shows among what agents decided while you were away, with **Overrule** to stop that agent and put the slice back. A slice you were working on in a chat of your own is left to you.

The slice turns amber and a card under **Your calls** says who stalled and on what, and it tells you once. A goal with nothing left but slices like this stops moving until someone takes one up. **Resume** starts that agent again, on its saved session if its chat closed, and gives it the slice back with where it stands now. **Hand on** gives the slice to another agent open in the project or to a new one, which runs the agent picked in Home's composer. The agent that takes it is told the goal, where the slice stands and what the stalled owner left: its branch and checkout, its commits, what it left uncommitted and the last thing it wrote. If that agent is signed out, Sikemux says so and starts nothing.

A chat that lost its goal's slice this way takes it back when it next runs a turn, unless someone took it meanwhile.

A goal with an agent on it and no slice, such as a card an agent claimed, has no gates or lease. Its mark follows the agent: a green ring while it works, a dashed grey ring while it sits idle. Once that agent's chat closes, the goal turns amber and **Your calls** has a card for it with **Resume** and **Hand on**, as for a slice.

An agent isn't stalled while a turn is running, however long, or while it waits on you, a check it asked for by marking the slice done, another card, a message, a process it started or agents it started, or while you have it open. A core restart starts every owner's 30 minutes again, and agents it interrupted carry on by themselves. After **Stop Everything** agents keep their slices for as long as they stay stopped, since you stopped them; the 30 minutes start again once you open or write to one.

## What a slice waits on

You never have to ask an agent for its status. Next and each slice's row say what the slice waits on now and, when Sikemux has an estimate, the time it should end by on your clock:

- **the project's check** on its owner's latest commit, here or in CI: queued, then running, such as "check · ETA 16:40" or "CI Release · ETA 16:46"
- **another card** it waits on, such as "waits for #4"
- **you**, at a gate only you pass
- **its owner**, in the middle of a turn, such as "Otter at work · ETA 17:10"

A check's estimate comes from how long the project's last few checks took since Sikemux last started; there is none until one has run. An owner's comes from how long the project's last slices took at that gate, once at least three have passed it. A wait that runs past its time says so, such as "check · was due 16:40". The pane changes the moment a wait starts, ends or runs over.

## When a goal should be through

A goal and its line on Home show when the work left in it should be through, such as "ETA 16:40". Sikemux works it out from its slices and shows one only when it has an estimate for all of them, so a slice nobody holds, or one whose owner isn't working on it, means no ETA. The time you take at your own gates doesn't count: "ETA 16:40, then you" means a gate only you pass comes after it, and once only you are left the ETA goes away.

When the ETA moves later than it first said, by more than two minutes or a tenth of how far off it was, the goal says why, such as "ETA slipped: CI failed, re-running, first said 16:40", and its time turns amber. The reason is one of a failed check, a new commit that has to be checked again, a slice added to the goal, another card or you holding a slice up, a slice with no owner, or work taking longer than it did before. When Sikemux has no new estimate, the goal says when it was due instead.

## A goal's report

A goal can report on itself, so you never have to ask its lead for status. Pick how often in the **Report** heading on the goal's page: **On change**, **Every 15 min**, **Every 30 min** or **Every hour**; **Off** stops it. Each report has the same rows every time:

- **Waiting on you**, first and amber when anything does, with the gate and since when
- **Done**: slices done since the report before, with when and by whom
- **Moving**: what is under way and what it waits on, such as "check · ETA 16:40"
- **Waiting on**: slices held up by another card, or with no owner
- **Next**: the next two gates and who passes them
- **ETA**: when the goal should be through and, when it moved later, by how much since the report before said and why

A dot marks what the report before didn't say. **‹** and **›** step through the goal's last twelve reports. On a timer a report is written even when nothing changed, but it tells you only when it says something new: the same notification as below, saying what waits on you, what got done and when the goal should be through. **On change** writes one at most once a minute, whenever a slice moves, lands or comes to wait on you.

## When a goal moves

A goal also tells you when it moves, in one short notification: slices landed, the goal finished or its ETA slipped, the same reasons in the same words. It only says something when something changed, never on a timer and never to say nothing changed. What moves within about 20 seconds comes as one notification, such as "2 slices landed". On your Mac it shows while Sikemux isn't in front, and clicking it opens the goal; over the workbench, where the goals are out of sight, it shows as a toast, and over Goals not at all. A phone hears it as a finished notification, unless Sikemux is open on it. Turn these off with **Notify when a goal moves** in **Settings → Notifications**. What needs you still notifies either way.

## Environments

**Environments**, under the project in the side rail, is where you say where that project deploys and who a deploy waits on. Only you can set them, since agents can write to the repository; they live in the core with the project's other board settings, not in `sikemux.json`. An agent can propose them, which shows as a card under **Your calls** and opens here for you to **Accept** or **Turn down**.

Each environment has a name, what Sikemux recognises it by, and who passes it:

| Passes | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Goes freely** | The deploy just goes. |
| **After the check** | It goes once the project's [check](/getting-started/projects/#the-projects-check) has passed; until then the agent is told to wait, and you aren't asked. |
| **A person** | It waits for you. |
| **Production** | It waits for you, always. |

Sikemux recognises an environment from hard facts, never a guess: a Rundeck job's path such as `dev/*`, Vercel's preview or production target, an AWS profile, a Kubernetes context, a Terraform workspace, or a command of the project's own by its first words. When several match, the strictest wins. **Set environments** starts with Dev, and **Add environment** goes on to Staging and Production, which you can rename, reorder or change.

What Sikemux can't recognise counts as production, and so does a command with something that could point it elsewhere, such as another kube config or credentials beside a profile. Nothing that reads as production can be marked to go without asking: not a name such as `prod-eu`, a Rundeck folder such as `prod/*`, or Vercel's production target. A project with none set keeps Built and Landed, and every deploy waits for you.

**Always allow** on a held deploy adds such an environment for you, marked **no step**: it only lets deploys go, so the project's slices climb what they climbed before. A project's own `ship` command with no word naming an environment, such as `./deploy.sh`, goes in one called Allowed, recognised by those first words. When the project's folder holds other projects, such as `~/proj/swishx` with a repository for each part of the product, it goes on that folder instead, and every project in it inherits it; a project's own command does so only when another project there lists it too. A project's own environments come first: the nearest that recognises a deploy decides, and among those the strictest wins, so a project that says `dev/*` waits for a person still waits. Only you can do this, and a job or command with a production word anywhere in it, such as `dev/promote-to-prod`, always waits. Change one the project holds here; one it inherits shows here with the folder it comes from, and removing it removes it for every project in that folder.

With environments set, the project's slices go **Built**, then a step for each environment in order, such as Dev, Staging, Production. Where you land slices yourself, **Landed** stays after Built. No slice or agent can add, skip or remove a step. A step passes when a deploy there has gone well on the slice's latest commit, and a new commit starts them over. A step that needs the check still needs it. Slices already done stay done.

Each deploy goes on the slice as evidence: what ran, where, why it did or didn't wait, and how it ended, with Rundeck's last log lines or Vercel's state. Give an environment a health address and Sikemux asks it once the deploy ends; a deploy whose health check fails counts as failed. A deploy that fails marks the slice failed and passes nothing. Where Sikemux can't see the end, as with a command that is only your project's own, it says so and the step passes on the deploy having gone.

At a step that waits for you, **Allow once** on the deploy, or **Pass Production** on the slice, lets it go, and the step passes once it has gone well. With no deploy waiting, passing it lets the agent deploy there once, and also serves for a deploy that happened elsewhere. You can also pass any waiting step whose needs are met yourself, for a project whose CI deploys on merge. An app too old to know environments shows these steps as Landed; **Allow once** still works there, and passing one asks you to update.

## A slice

Click a slice to open it, or right-click one with an owner for that agent's menu. Its owner's name opens their chat, and the button by its title opens it beside the agent board. Under its promise and owner, the ladder shows each gate with who passed it and when, or who may pass it and what it still needs. Under the ladder, each of the goal's assertions the slice fulfils is ticked once its evidence held up; an assertion it named that no longer stands on its goal, such as one retired, shows struck through as freed. Each thumbnail of evidence says which assertions it was collected for, by number.

The evidence for the gate waiting leads, large, a picture first when there is one; the rest sit as thumbnails under it, and clicking one brings it to the front. The eye on a picture shows it full size over Goals, and clicking a recording opens it in the workbench's viewer. A change lists the files it touches; click one to open it, or the line above them to open Git. A run's output scrolls. The checks Sikemux ran fold into one line. When a slice's change touches a web page, Sikemux takes [the changed page](/getting-started/projects/#the-changed-page) itself once the slice passes **Built**, and those pictures name the address they opened.

When the slice waits on you, the gate's own button passes it, such as **Landed** or **Pass Production**. **Needs work** opens a line to say what is wrong. The slice then waits on its owner, not you, until the owner marks it done again. Whether its work looks right isn't asked of the slice: its goal asks once for all of them ([looking once at a goal](#looking-once-at-a-goal)), and a note written while the goal waits for that is the goal's **Needs work**.

### Changes

Whatever you write on a slice is a change for its agent to make. Click a spot on the picture in front to pin one there, or click the line under the list to write one for the whole slice; either way it is pinned to the evidence in front and goes to the agent whose work that evidence shows. **Changes** lists them, open ones first, each with its number, which also marks its spot on the picture: amber while open, green once ticked.

The agent ticks each one off when it is made, saying what it did and naming the evidence that shows it; a change pinned to a picture needs a screenshot or recording taken since. The proof shows on the row, and clicking its thumbnail brings that evidence to the front. **Built** doesn't pass while a change is open, and the review checks every change and how it was ticked. **Not done** on a ticked change reopens it and sends the slice back to its agent. The rules every agent on the slice keeps fold under the list.
