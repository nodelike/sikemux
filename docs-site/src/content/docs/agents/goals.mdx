A goal is a card at the top of the [agent board](/agents/board/) with slices under it. A slice at the top, with no goal above it, such as a card Auto runs, is a goal of its own with one slice. **Goals** is a view of its own in the middle of the window: it puts what needs you first, then each goal and its slices, for the project you pick. Switch to it with the goals button at the top right of the window, after the clock and beside focus mode, or press `⌘⌥7`; press either again to go back to the workbench. The window keeps whichever you left it on.

Both rails stay. The side rail keeps your projects, hosts and the rest, with **Home** and **Overnight** at its top, each with how many things need you or happened while you were away; picking a project there shows that project's goals. The rail on the right lists only that project's goals, each with the mark of where it stands and how many things in it need you; click one to open it.

## Starting work

The composer at the bottom of Home, the same one a chat has, asks what should get done in the project in front of the workbench. Write as you would to an agent: add files with **+**, drop files, folders or pictures anywhere on Home, paste screenshots, mention agents with `@`, issues with `#`, pull requests with `!` and files with `+`, dictate with the microphone, and use the up arrow to bring back what you started before. What you write is kept as you type, so it is still there after a restart. The Stash and slash commands belong to a chat's session, so they show in the chat once it starts. Pick how it starts with the switch at the end of its bar; hover a choice to see what it does. **Hand off** gives it to an agent that works on it on its own, out of sight, **Plan first** adds a goal and starts a lead that talks it through with you and proposes slices for you to approve, and **Chat** starts a chat with you, kept as a goal. The composer opens on **Chat**. While you type, Sikemux switches to **Plan first** when the text calls for it and marks it with a dot; hover it to see why, such as when the text touches server code, a migration, the protocol, what people see, or a path your project lists under [`planFirst`](/getting-started/projects/). Otherwise it stays on **Chat**; it never picks **Hand off** for you. You can always pick another. The agent menu picks which agent starts, its account and its model. Enter or the send arrow starts it, and the agent gets what you wrote with your files and mentions. That agent holds its card from the start. **Plan first** and **Chat** open the new chat right here in Goals.

**Hand off** starts its agent in the background, in a checkout of its own cut from where the project is now, and keeps its chat out of your way: the work shows under **Moving** on Home, and under **Needs you** when it asks you something, its turn fails, or it waits at a gate only you pass. Its card is a slice whose promise is what you typed, cut to one line of 200 characters if it is longer, with the whole text in its details. The agent commits and lands its work on the branch it was cut from, and the slice is **Built** once the project's [check](/getting-started/projects/#the-projects-check) passes on it there, which Sikemux runs itself. A project that names no check has nothing to show the work **Built**, so the agent asks you how it is to be checked and the slice waits at **Built** until it has one. Open the agent's chat from its line under **Moving** whenever you want to look in. Right after you hand it off, the line under the composer names its card for a few seconds; **Open** shows it.

## What needs you

Goals opens on **Home**, laid out like a new chat: the agent asks what you're making in the project you picked, with what needs you in any project and what is moving in this one under it, and the composer at the bottom. **Waiting on you** comes first: one line for each agent, in any project, that ended its turn asking you something, with the question, the agent, its project and how long it has waited. When the question takes a short answer, such as **Yes** or **No** to an agent offering to do more, its answers sit on the line and one click gives yours; when the agent asked several things, the line then shows the next, and the answers go back together once each has one; click anywhere else on the line to open the agent's chat at the question and reply in your own words. **Needs you** lists the rest of what waits on you in any project, grouped by what you're needed for: **Decide** (a gate to pass, a permission, a pull request, a ticket closed with nothing landed, or an agent's push, deploy or other action that [leaves this machine](/agents/permissions/#what-leaves-this-machine), shown with its exact command and **Allow once** and **Refuse** beside it), **Unblock** (a sign-in, a secret, a terminal waiting for you to type) and **Fix** (a failed check or something held). Each line names its goal or agent, its project and how long it has waited. Click it to open what it is about: an agent's assumption, permission or wait for typing opens its chat, a pull request opens its page, and anything else on a slice or goal opens that slice or goal here, bringing its project to the front. A card that is neither opens beside the project's [agent board](/agents/board/), and a line with no card opens the agent's chat. An agent's chat opens right here in Goals, the same live chat as the agent's tab in the workbench, which stays open; the link at the top of the chat, `Esc` once no turn is left to stop, or `⌘W` goes back to where you were, and `⌘⌥7` shows that tab in the workbench. Anywhere else in Goals `⌘W` does nothing, since no tab is showing to close. An agent that runs in a terminal has no chat, so its line opens its tab in the workbench, which takes Goals' place, as the board does; `⌘⌥7` brings Goals back. Right-click a line about an agent for the same menu its tab has: rename, sleep, close, share and the rest. The count beside **Home** counts the same things. **Moving** lists the goals with work under way, each with a strip of its slices, and **Done** the goals whose slices have all passed. Click a goal to open it.

Whatever comes to need you also tells you, once: a notification on your Mac while Sikemux isn't in front, and on your phone as its notification settings say. That covers a gate to pass, a card handed back, an owner that stalled, agents stuck on each other, knowledge that no longer holds and a turn that ends asking you something, besides the permissions, questions and problems that always did. It doesn't tell you again about the same thing, or while you have that agent open, and the notification goes away once it no longer needs you.

When things finished or failed while you were away, a line above what needs you says how many, and opens **Overnight**: how many finished, failed and wait on you, then each event in time order, grouped by goal. Each event opens its slice or its agent's chat.

## A goal

The goal shows who leads it, whose name opens their chat, and which agents own its slices, its ticket with a link to it in its tracker, a button that opens it beside the [agent board](/agents/board/), where everything about its card can be changed, a strip with one segment per slice, and **Next**: the gates its slices wait at, in the order they will be reached. **Needs you** lists the slices waiting at a gate only you pass. **Slices** lists every slice with what it waits on, its ticket and its owner.

**Assertions** lists what must hold once the goal is done, each with the kind of evidence that shows it (the check passing, a recording, a screenshot, or a CI workflow passing, named on the line as "CI Build passes") and how many of the slices that fulfil it have that evidence. Add one on the line under them, picking its kind; **CI workflow** asks for the workflow's name. Retire one with **×**.

**Slices land** sets who passes Landed in the project: **Once on the branch**, which Sikemux passes, or **When you pass it**, where nothing counts as landed until you pass Landed yourself ([when slices land](/agents/board/#when-slices-land)).

**Rules** lists the rules the goal's agents follow: the goal's own, then those Sikemux keeps for the project and the folders above it ([what Sikemux remembers](/agents/memory/)). **Move to AGENTS.md** moves a rule kept for the project into its repository's `AGENTS.md`, where every agent reads it with the code, and Sikemux stops keeping it. The file is left uncommitted for you to review. The button shows only on a rule that can move: never a sensitive one, which never goes into the repository, nor one kept for a folder that isn't the top of a git checkout, or whose `AGENTS.md` links outside it.

Every slice wears the same mark: solid green when done, a green ring while it moves, a dashed grey ring while it waits, amber when it needs you and red when its latest check failed. A goal wears the mark of its slices together: red when one failed, amber when one needs you, a green ring when one moves, solid green when every one is done, and a dashed grey ring otherwise.

## When an owner stalls

Holding a slice is a lease, so the team never waits in silence on an agent that has gone. A slice loses its owner when the agent's chat closes, or when the agent has run no turn and left the slice untouched for 30 minutes, whether it sits idle, asleep or stopped. It keeps its gates and its work, and anyone can take it up: another agent on the team, or Auto where it takes the card.

The slice turns amber and **Needs you** lists it under **Unblock**, saying who stalled and on what, and it tells you once. **Resume** starts that agent again, on its saved session if its chat closed, and gives it the slice back. **Hand on** gives the slice to another agent open in the project or to a new one.

An agent isn't stalled while a turn is running, however long, or while it waits on you, a check, another card, a message, a process it started or agents it started, or while you have it open. A core restart starts every owner's 30 minutes again, and agents it interrupted carry on by themselves. After **Stop Everything** agents keep their slices for as long as they stay stopped, since you stopped them; the 30 minutes start again once you open or write to one.

## What a slice waits on

You never have to ask an agent for its status. Next and each slice's row say what the slice waits on now and, when Sikemux has an estimate, the time it should end by on your clock:

- **the project's check** on its owner's latest commit, here or in CI: queued, then running, such as "check · ETA 16:40" or "CI Release · ETA 16:46"
- **another card** it waits on, such as "waits for #4"
- **you**, at a gate only you pass
- **its owner**, in the middle of a turn, such as "Otter at work · ETA 17:10"

A check's estimate comes from how long the project's last few checks took since Sikemux last started; there is none until one has run. An owner's comes from how long the project's last slices took at that gate, once at least three have passed it. A wait that runs past its time says so, such as "check · was due 16:40". The pane changes the moment a wait starts, ends or runs over.

## When a goal should be through

A goal and its line under **Moving** show when the work left in it should be through, such as "ETA 16:40". Sikemux works it out from its slices and shows one only when it has an estimate for all of them, so a slice nobody holds, or one whose owner isn't working on it, means no ETA. The time you take at your own gates doesn't count: "ETA 16:40, then you" means a gate only you pass comes after it, and once only you are left the ETA goes away.

When the ETA moves later than it first said, by more than two minutes or a tenth of how far off it was, the goal says why, such as "ETA slipped: CI failed, re-running, first said 16:40", and its time turns amber. The reason is one of a failed check, a new commit that has to be checked again, a slice added to the goal, another card or you holding a slice up, a slice with no owner, or work taking longer than it did before. When Sikemux has no new estimate, the goal says when it was due instead.

## When a goal moves

A goal also tells you when it moves, in one short notification: slices landed, the goal finished or its ETA slipped, the same reasons in the same words. It only says something when something changed, never on a timer and never to say nothing changed. What moves within about 20 seconds comes as one notification, such as "2 slices landed". On your Mac it shows while Sikemux isn't in front, and clicking it opens the goal; over the workbench, where the goals are out of sight, it shows as a toast, and over Goals not at all. A phone hears it as a finished notification, unless Sikemux is open on it. Turn these off with **Notify when a goal moves** in **Settings → Notifications**. What needs you still notifies either way.

## A slice

Click a slice to open it, or right-click one with an owner for that agent's menu. Its owner's name opens their chat, and the button by its title opens it beside the agent board. Under its promise and owner, the ladder shows each gate with who passed it and when, or who may pass it and what it still needs. Under the ladder, each of the goal's assertions the slice fulfils is ticked once its evidence held up; an assertion it named that no longer stands on its goal, such as one retired, shows struck through as freed. Each thumbnail of evidence says which assertions it was collected for, by number.

The evidence for the gate waiting leads, large, a picture first when there is one; the rest sit as thumbnails under it, and clicking one brings it to the front. Clicking a picture shows it full size over Goals, and clicking a recording opens it in the workbench's viewer. A change lists the files it touches; click one to open it, or the line above them to open Git. A run's output scrolls. The checks Sikemux ran fold into one line. When a slice's change touches a web page, Sikemux takes [the changed page](/getting-started/projects/#the-changed-page) itself once the slice passes **Built**, and those pictures name the address they opened.

When the slice waits on you, **Looks right** passes the gate. **Needs work** opens a line to say what is wrong; the note is pinned to the evidence in front and goes to the agent whose work it shows. Notes are listed under the buttons, newest first.
