---
title: What Sikemux remembers
description: "Rules, knowledge and decisions you say once, which the agents started in the project are handed."
---

Sikemux keeps what you would otherwise repeat in every chat, so you say it once and the agents started in the project are handed it. It keeps three kinds of thing, each at one level:

| Kind          | Kept for                                  | Example                                | Kept until                                 |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Rules**     | You, the project, or one goal             | "Shadows, never glows"                 | Someone retires it, or its goal is removed |
| **Knowledge** | You, or the project                       | "The service is called ai-service now" | It is proven wrong                         |
| **Decisions** | The project, marked with the goal it was for | "One project is one concept"        | Someone retires it                         |

Rules and knowledge kept for you are in your own store, which every project reads: how you like to work everywhere, rather than how one project does. Tell an agent how you like to work everywhere, and it keeps that as yours. The project's own store follows its repository, so every checkout of it reads the same one, and it is the same on every computer of yours that holds the project: one added on a linked computer is kept at the project's home.

A rule or knowledge can move between yours and the project's from its row on the [Playbook](/agents/playbook/#rules). Knowledge tested by a path inside the project stays with the project.

Agents keep rules and knowledge with the `memory_add`, `memory_update` and `memory_list` tools, and decisions with `decide`, when you tell them to remember something or when they find it out. Each one says which agent learned it, in which chat and when, so you can check where it came from. A correction keeps what it said before. The project keeps one list of decisions, each marked with the goal the agent was working toward, or with none when it had no goal; past 512 it drops its oldest retired ones first. A goal keeps its own rules on its card, 64 of them, dropping its oldest retired ones first. A goal is a card with no card above it, and once it has decisions or rules of its own it can't be moved under another card.

A note only reaches the agents that read it. So when an agent saves a rule or knowledge about code, Sikemux asks it to add a lint or a land check that enforces it, or to say why no check can.

## What an agent is handed

When another agent starts an agent in a project, its task ends with what Sikemux keeps, in the same message:

- every rule of yours and of the project
- only the knowledge its task names: knowledge that names paths or services goes to agents whose task or card names one of them as a whole word, and knowledge that names none goes to every agent.
- the project's decisions its task names, or else the newest ones of its goal.
- once the agent holds a task, the goal the task sits under: the goal's own title and details and its rules. An agent started by another agent works toward its lead's goal, so it gets the goal's part before it claims a task of its own.

Nobody writes a handoff by hand. A new agent handed a card, by **Resume** or by taking its chat up again from **Recent**, is told, from the owner that lost it, the branch and checkout it worked in, its commits there, what it left uncommitted and the last thing it wrote, so it carries the work on rather than starting again.

A chat you start yourself is handed what Sikemux keeps once, as it starts: every rule of yours and of the project, the knowledge that names nothing in particular, and the project's decisions your first message names. Claude Code and Codex take it among their instructions, after any developer instructions of your own in Codex's config; other agents get it with your first message that isn't a slash command, shown there as context rather than as your words. It holds what Sikemux kept when the chat began; what changes later, and a goal's rules once the agent holds a card, it reads with `memory_list`.

Rules and knowledge an agent wrote come under their own heading, naming the agent and saying they are notes, not your orders, so a rule an agent picked up from a web page can't pass as yours. A goal's decisions name the agent that made them.

What Sikemux keeps stays out of the agent CLIs' own memory, so it is the same for every agent, account and CLI.

## Rules from what you say

When you tell an agent how agents should always or never work in the project, it keeps that as your rule, in your words, without asking. The chat shows one quiet line, **Kept as a rule · Undo**; Undo drops it again. Once you remove it, from that line or the Playbook, the line reads **Kept as a rule, then undone**, and the agent hears with its next turn that you removed it. Your rules, and what agents noted apart from them, are listed on the project's [Playbook](/agents/playbook/).

## Sensitive entries

A rule or knowledge marked sensitive, such as a host or an address, never goes into the repository. Agents in the project are still handed it, since that is what it is for, so it shows in their chats. Where Sikemux lists what it keeps, only its own window shows sensitive entries; phones and other programs see how many were left out, not what they say, and the inbox says only that sensitive knowledge no longer holds.

## Projects shared with an org

Once you share a project with an org you belong to, its rules are the org's too: every teammate at the org who has the project gets them, and their agents are handed them. An org's rules apply to a project only after you share it with that org yourself. The Rules page lists the org's rules, naming the teammate who added each while they are a member, and no one once they leave or delete their account.

Rules that stay with you are marked **Only you**. Only a rule you type on the Playbook goes to the org. One kept from a chat, made by an Overrule or picked from past sessions stays **Only you** until you move it with **Move to** and the org's name on its row. So does a sensitive rule, or one that looks like a key or a password.

The org's rules are kept sealed in its vault. Until the vault is set up on this computer, the project's rules stay **Only you** and the Rules page says so. An owner's or admin's computer sets the vault up once a project is shared, and a computer waiting for it opens it once one of your hosts that has it, or an owner's or admin's host, is online.

An org can have its admins approve rules first, on its Policy page at [app.sikemux.com](https://app.sikemux.com). Then a rule you add or move goes to the org's admins, who are asked to keep or drop it. Its row reads **Waits for an admin**, with **Take back**, and **dropped by** and the admin's name if they drop it. A sensitive rule never waits for them: it stays **Only you**, and its row says to ask an owner or admin to add it. Moving a rule the org keeps already, or adding one while the waiting list is full, is refused and the row says why. Rules still waiting join the org's once the org lets every member add, and go back to **Only you** if the project stops being shared with it. Rules written while offline, before the org switched to approvals, come back to your own store, saying why.

When the org won't take a rule or a change, such as when it has reached a cap, the rule stays on this computer and its row says why: **Only you, Acme didn't take it** and the reason.

## When knowledge no longer holds

Knowledge can be proven wrong two ways.

- **Someone says so.** You, or an agent that runs into the opposite, either corrects it with what is so instead, or says it no longer holds without knowing what does.
- **Sikemux tests it.** Knowledge can say how to test it: a path exists, or a host answers on a port. Sikemux tests it every six hours, and soon after an agent was handed it on a test more than ten minutes old. An agent never waits for a test; it is handed what the last one found. A host that can't be reached at all, such as an internal one while the VPN is off, proves nothing, so the knowledge stays as it was; only a host that refuses the connection counts against it.

Knowledge that failed its test, or that someone said no longer holds, is left out of what agents are handed and shows in the inbox as something to fix. Correct it or retire it. Knowledge that only failed its test holds again by itself once the test passes.
