---
title: Linked hosts
description: "Agents that start workers on your other hosts, and the sign-ins they use there."
---

Every host signed in to your account links with every other on its own, so an agent on one starts workers on another. A host that turned on **Require approval for new devices** asks you first. Removing a host from your account unlinks it everywhere.

A worker on another host runs there on that host's own sign-in for its CLI, in a checkout of its own. Its commits come back to a branch in the lead's checkout each time its turn ends.

## Sign-ins

An agent uses the sign-ins of the host it runs on, whichever host started or messaged it: that host's [connectors](/agents/connectors/) such as Jira, GitHub, AWS or Slack, its saved databases and its browser tabs. A worker on another host gets that host's sign-ins, never this one's. Sign-ins never leave the host they were made on.

## Sending a project to another host

**Settings → General → Session transfer** sends the project in front to another host you've linked, with its chats and tabs. Pick the host, then:

- **Copy** leaves the project here as it is.
- **Move** closes its chats here and removes their saved conversations, but only once the other host has every chat and tab. If anything doesn't arrive whole, nothing here changes.

Tick **Bring uncommitted changes** to take the checkout's uncommitted work along. Chats come along when their agent keeps them in files, as Claude Code and Codex do; the others stay here. A chat that came along runs on the other host's sign-in the next time you send to it. Sikemux then says what went and what stayed, and opens the project on the other host.

## The Hosts page

The **Hosts** page shows how busy each of your hosts is. Open it with the gauge beside **+** on the side rail's **Hosts** header, with **Hosts** in the command palette or under **New…**, or with ⌘⇧H. To open it on one host, pick **Host details** at the top of the **⋯** beside that host, or beside **This host**. Clicking a host's row still switches the window to that host.

When a host runs short of memory or disk, stays busy on every core, or goes out of reach, it says so under **Your calls** in Goals, at the top of the notch's list, and in a coloured line under its name in the side rail, such as **Memory 98%** or **Out of reach 14 min**. Click any of them to open the Hosts page on that host.
