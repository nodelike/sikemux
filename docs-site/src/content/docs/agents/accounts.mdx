---
title: Accounts and limits
description: "Several Claude, Codex or Antigravity accounts, and how much of a plan is used."
---

Claude Code, Codex and, on a Mac or Linux, Antigravity can keep several accounts, such as one for work and one for yourself. Each account is a separate folder for the CLI's own sign-in. Sikemux never stores your credentials; to read plan limits (after first-run setup), it uses each CLI's own sign-in only against its vendor's usage endpoint.

## Add an account

In **Settings → Agents**, open Claude's or Codex's row and choose **Add** under **Accounts**. In the box that opens over the window, **Add an account for Claude**, give it a name, then sign in in your browser. Once it is signed in, **Switch to** it, or **Keep using** the account you had. Each account in the list has **Use** to switch to it, which moves your open chats there too, and its **⋯** menu signs it out or removes it.

When adding one fails, the box shows the error with **Try again**. An agent that can keep only one account says so in place of the form.

An **Antigravity** account is added the same way, or with the **+** in the limits card or the model picker: open Google's link, choose the account, and paste the code the page shows. Its sign-in is kept as a file in a private folder of its own, so the account `agy` is signed in to on your Mac stays as it is. Removing the account signs it out and deletes that folder. Windows keeps one Antigravity account for now.

## Move between accounts

**Move between accounts** is on by default, for Claude and Codex, and only acts once an agent has two or more signed-in accounts. Each new Claude chat then starts on the account whose limit for its model refills soonest, its model's own week where the plan has one, else the plan's week, since what a week leaves unused is lost at its reset; accounts that refill within an hour of each other go to the one with more left. An account takes a new chat only while its 5-hour window is under 80% used and would not fill before it resets at the pace its running chats use it, its week, and the week of the chat's model where the plan has one, are under 95%, and it has room for a typical chat of that project. What is left counts by plan size, so half a Max week counts as more than half a Pro week. When no account has room, the chat starts on the account you chose, as with **Move between accounts** off. A new chat shows which account it will start on and why, marked Smart pick, under its activity map before you send anything.

Saved limit resets are kept for when they are needed. Sikemux spends one by itself only when no account has room and the next refill is further away than a week divided by the number of accounts; until then the chat waits for the refill, and the limits panel says **waiting for refill**, how long the reset is kept and what using it now would give back. In a reset's last week, the account that holds it takes that model's chats first, so it isn't lost.

A chat that hits a usage limit moves on in the same order, to the account that refills first and has room. When every account it may use is out, it waits and sends your last message again as soon as Sikemux reads room on one of them, or when the limit resets at the latest. A lead whose worker waits so only hears that Sikemux paused it and resumes it by itself, never the limit or the account. A chat already going never moves for balance alone: its cache lives with its account, and moving it would send its whole conversation again. When a big Claude chat does move, by a limit or by your pick, it goes on in a fresh session that starts from a brief of where it stands (its latest exchanges word for word, recent decisions and work in hand) rather than sending the whole conversation to the new account. A small chat carries on in its own session.

Antigravity accounts never switch by themselves once a chat is going. With **Move between accounts** on, a new chat, agents' workers included, starts on the account with the most Gemini room left in its 5-hour and weekly limits, marked Smart pick, and the shared Claude and GPT limit doesn't count. When one runs out its chat offers the others with what each has left, such as **Carry on with Work · 80% left**, one tap each.

To keep a project to some accounts, such as a work project off your personal account, open an account's **⋯** menu in its agent's row in **Settings → Agents** and untick the projects under **New chats here in**. A project then starts new chats only on the accounts left ticked for it, and a chat in it carries on at a limit only on those.

To turn it off, switch off **Move between accounts** in the **Accounts** section of **Settings → Agents**. Chats then start on the account you chose and wait for a limit to reset. Check each provider's terms first: they expect one person's ordinary use per plan, and rotating several paid accounts may not be within them. A chat never moves to another agent on its own. When every account it may use is out of usage and another agent you are signed in to has room, the limit line offers **Hand to** that agent, such as **Hand to Codex**, which carries the chat on there as [handing a chat to another agent](/agents/chats/#handing-a-chat-to-another-agent) does.

## Unpaid or signed-out accounts

When a plan is unpaid or past due, a turn on it fails with "plan is unpaid" and offers another account, and new chats stop starting there until a turn goes through on it again. When a provider refuses an account this way, or because it was signed out, you get one ask, at the bell, on the notch and on your phone, naming the chats it stopped. Move them to the account with the most room or to another one, retry once you have paid or signed in again, or leave them stopped. A lead can also move its agents' chats to another account with the `agent_account` tool.

## Choose a chat's account

With two or more signed-in accounts, the line under a chat's composer shows the chat's account at its right end. Pick another there: a new or sleeping chat starts on it when you begin writing, and a chat already going moves to it, carrying on any turn in progress. The menu lists only accounts that can take the chat, by name and never by email, and says why when none can, such as when the others are signed out. With **Move between accounts** on, it tags **Smart** on the account it would pick for the chat's model. If a move fails, the chat stays on its account and Sikemux says why.

## Limits

The foot of the tab rail's agents page lists every account of the agents shown, for Claude and Codex together, or just the agent chosen in the rail. Each row is the account's avatar with its agent's mark on the corner, its name, and a ruler for its five-hour **Session** with a hairline under it for its **Week**, or its **Week** alone when its plan has no session limit, and how much of each is used. Every ruler is the same length, so the accounts compare at a glance; a ruler fills as the limit is used, and it and its number turn amber, then red, as it runs low. The header counts the limit resets the accounts hold.

Rest the pointer on the rows and a card opens above them with each agent's accounts under its name, every ruler lined up; an agent with one account shows its plan beside that name. Each account has its avatar, its name and its plan as its agent names it (Claude's Ultra, Codex's Pro), then a ruler for each limit its plan has (Session, Week, and a model's own week such as Fable), how much of each is used and the countdown to each reset. Under them, **Resets** lists each kind the account holds with how many, such as Weekly ×1 and Fable ×3, and the day it must be used by, amber within a month and red within a week. A reset Claude holds back for now, such as for a while after one was used, still counts and says **Not yet** in place of **Use**. **Use** opens the row to say what spending one does, such as refilling the whole week from 74% used back to 0%; **Reset week** spends it and the numbers are read again, and **Cancel** leaves it. With **Move between accounts** on, it chooses where each new chat starts. With it off, **Move chats here** on another account puts the agent on it and moves your open chats there too; new chats keep starting there until you pick another. **Undo**, for a few seconds, takes the pick back and puts each chat it moved on the account it was on. Chats running in a terminal stay where they are. With every agent listed, each has a **+** in the float to add an account; with one agent, the **+** is beside the refresh button. A signed-out account offers **Sign in**.

After first-run setup finishes, Sikemux reads your limits when it starts, every five minutes while the panel is on screen, and whenever the window comes forward or the card opens on a reading more than 20 seconds old, as claude.ai does; the refresh button reads them again now. For Claude Code, Sikemux reads the OAuth token Claude saved to ask Anthropic's usage endpoint (and reset endpoint when you spend a reset) and keeps it nowhere; for Codex, it asks `codex app-server`. A failed read keeps the last numbers and says how old they are.

Grok, Antigravity and OpenCode Go show their limits too, under their own rows, once they have some to show: Grok's credit for its billing period when you signed in with `grok login`, Antigravity's 5-hour and weekly limits as `agy`'s own `/usage` prints them, and OpenCode Go's session, week and month when OpenCode keeps an OpenCode Go key, including one added in OpenCode 2. For Grok and OpenCode Go, Sikemux reads the sign-in token its CLI saved only to ask that vendor's own usage endpoint, and keeps it nowhere; for Antigravity, it runs `agy -p /usage`. An API key, or a Grok set up to sign in another way, has no plan limits, so it has no row.

An account you signed out of stays in its agent's list, where you can sign in to it again or remove it.

## Where tokens go

**Settings › Usage** counts what agents run inside Sikemux used, priced at API rates; a subscription bills separately. **Tokens** splits what agents read back from the prompt cache from what they wrote to it, and **Cost** says how much went on cache writes, which is where resuming a cold chat or rotating an account spends. Under **By model**, each model has a bar of where its cost went: cache write, output, cache read and input, each with its amount. **Share week**, beside **Overview**, makes a picture of the last 7 days: the share each model took, commits and agent hours, with no costs, emails or repository names. **Save PNG** writes it to a file and **Copy image** puts it on the clipboard.

Each time one chat's cost passes another $50, Sikemux tells you with an ask that needs no answer. It never stops the agent.

## Which program runs

Open an agent's row in **Settings → Agents** and its **Runs** line shows the program the account in use runs, such as `claude from PATH`. **Change** sets another path; leave it empty to use the one on your `PATH`.
